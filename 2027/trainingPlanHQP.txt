## Training environment and culture

I hope to foster an environment with a sense of excitement at the adventure of research, balanced by a sense of responsibility to produce useful research that directly supports informed decision making toward sustainable decisions. A culture of open collaboration within the group but also with the broader community. A spirit of indepedent, critical and creative thinking that is solution oriented. And open to interdisciplinary innovation.

As current PhD students complete their work, this will involve recruiting about six to seven new HQP during the funding period—approximately two-thirds PhD and one-third MSc, with one or two undergraduate trainees. PhD students will focus on developing transformational methodological innovations at the frontier of industrial ecology modelling, while MSc students will contribute to innovative implementation and validation within applied case studies.



TRAINING STRUCTURE AND ACTIVITIES.
This plan builds directly on mentoring practices that have proven successful in my group. Each HQP benefits from weekly individual meetings devoted to scientific progress, problem-solving, and career development. I also lead two thematic discussion groups (one on scientific software development and another monthly seminar on circular-economy research) where students present methods and results to peers. With my team, we've already discussed adding a yearly team-building retreat in nature to help reinforce group cohesion and well-being.
These structured interactions ensure that HQP develop not only technical expertise but also the confidence to communicate and collaborate across disciplines and sectors.

Beyond project work, students are supported through a comprehensive training ecosystem covering:

- scientific writing through workshops, shared draft reviews, and iterative feedback;

- software development and programming best practices;

- reproducible and transparent, code-based research workflows;

- oral-presentation boot camps;

- participation in proposal preparation, peer review, and the development of teaching material.

These initiatives will continue to evolve as the team grows, ensuring a consistent yet adaptive framework for skill development.

SKILL DEVELOPMENT.
HQP will acquire advanced technical expertise in sustainability modelling, data integration, scenario analysis, and uncertainty assessment, along with professional skills in teamwork, communication (especially with non-scientific partners), project management, and writing. Training in open-science methods (version control, documentation, reproducibility) remains a cornerstone of my group’s identity. Together, these skills equip students to contribute to both scientific innovation and evidence-based decision-making in sustainability transitions.

CAREER PREPARATION AND MENTORSHIP.
Each HQP is guided to define a personal research trajectory and professional path. Students are encouraged to publish as first authors, present at international conferences, and apply for scholarships and travel grants. The objective is to prepare HQP for diverse roles in academia, government, and industry, supported by a deep understanding of the scientific and social dimensions of sustainability research.

All students receive financial support above institutional minimums.

HQP TRAINING PLAN

Over the next five years, I will maintain and further strengthen a research group of 7–10 HQP under my direct supervision, complemented by several co-supervised students. As current PhD students complete their work, this will involve recruiting about six to seven new HQP during the funding period, approximately two-thirds PhD and one-third MSc, with one or two undergraduate trainees. This size allows for individualized mentorship while sustaining an intellectually vibrant team. PhD students will focus on developing transformational methodological innovations at the frontier of industrial ecology and sustainability modelling, while MSc students will contribute to innovative implementation and validation within applied case studies. Undergraduate trainees will receive a first exposure to research, data processing, and reproducible coding practices, often as contributors to shared group tools.

This steady-state structure ensures continuity between research projects and students’ development, allowing incoming HQP to benefit from the expertise, tools, and mentoring culture already in place. The approach builds directly on the methods that have proven most effective in my past supervision and that have yielded strong HQP outcomes.

TRAINING STRUCTURE AND ACTIVITIES

Each HQP benefits from weekly one-on-one meetings devoted to scientific progress, problem solving, and career development. These sessions are the cornerstone of my supervision, offering space for candid reflection, technical feedback, and forward planning. I also lead two thematic discussion groups, one focused on scientific software development and another monthly seminar on circular-economy research, where students present methods, exchange challenges, and propose solutions to one another’s problems. These recurring dialogues have become a key part of our lab culture, fostering intellectual curiosity, collective problem solving, and the ability to articulate and defend research ideas among peers.

Collaborative code and data reviews, already implemented for several projects, will be systematically expanded to strengthen open-science practices. Twice yearly, students present their work at ShareCIRAIG internal symposia, where they receive feedback from faculty, researchers, and industrial partners, and participate in CIRAIG partner meetings, where they learn to communicate research results in applied contexts. Building on recent discussions within the group, I plan to add a yearly team-building retreat in nature to reinforce group cohesion, reflection, and well-being.

These structured interactions ensure that HQP develop not only technical expertise but also confidence in communication, teamwork, and interdisciplinary collaboration. They also foster a sense of belonging and shared purpose, key ingredients for long-term student success.

TRAINING ECOSYSTEM

Beyond project-specific supervision, students are supported through a comprehensive and evolving training ecosystem that provides structured learning opportunities in
- scientific writing through workshops, shared draft reviews, and iterative feedback
- software development and programming best practices
- reproducible and transparent, code-based research workflows
- oral-presentation and communication training, including practice sessions before major conferences
- active participation in research funding and dissemination, such as contributing to proposals, reviewing abstracts, and developing teaching materials

These initiatives will continue to evolve as the team grows, with new student-led seminars and peer mentoring planned to promote autonomy and leadership. Together, they provide a consistent yet adaptive framework that ensures HQP gain the skills and confidence to operate independently as scientists.

SKILL DEVELOPMENT AND KNOWLEDGE TRANSLATION

Through their work on this program, HQP will gain advanced expertise in sustainability modelling, data integration, scenario analysis, and uncertainty assessment, along with professional skills in teamwork, communication (particularly with non-scientific partners), project management, and scientific writing. Training in open-science methods such as version control, documentation, and reproducibility is a defining feature of my group and will remain at its core. These practices enable HQP to conduct research that is not only rigorous but also transparent, auditable, and reusable, directly contributing to Canada’s leadership in open, data-driven sustainability science. By the end of their program, HQP will be able to translate complex environmental models into actionable insights for policy and industry. This integration of technical mastery with societal relevance is a central goal of the training environment.

CAREER PREPARATION AND MENTORSHIP

Each HQP is guided to define a personal research trajectory and long-term career plan. Students are encouraged to publish as first authors, present at international conferences, and apply for scholarships and travel grants. They gain teaching and supervision experience through undergraduate projects and frequent interaction with industry analysts and sustainability practitioners, helping them connect theoretical research with practical decision-making contexts.

Mentorship also extends beyond the research itself. I maintain a transparent dialogue about the realities of academic and professional life, including funding processes, collaboration dynamics, and workload management, so that trainees understand both the opportunities and the constraints of research careers. These discussions help normalize the challenges of scientific work, reinforcing resilience and confidence. The overall objective is to prepare HQP for diverse and impactful careers in academia, government, and industry, equipped with both technical depth and systemic understanding.

EQUITY, DIVERSITY, AND INCLUSION

I will sustain and strengthen EDI practices already established in my group. Recruitment is conducted through open calls with anonymized CVs in the first screening round and at least one gender-diverse committee member. I foster a culture of openness where students can discuss personal or mental-health challenges without stigma. Flexible schedules and remote work are standard, and all HQP are encouraged to take a minimum of four to six weeks of vacation each year to maintain balance. Collaboration with leading female, francophone, and international scientists within CIRAIG provides visible role models and diverse mentorship perspectives.

To ensure accountability, an independent analyst conducts annual confidential interviews with trainees to gather feedback on inclusivity and team dynamics. These measures have already proven effective in fostering openness and mutual respect, and will be maintained, strengthened, and periodically reviewed to ensure continuous improvement. All students receive financial support above institutional minimums, ensuring equitable access to research opportunities regardless of personal means.

My long-term vision is to sustain an environment that combines the excitement of discovery with a strong sense of responsibility toward societal impact. The lab culture values curiosity, collaboration, and openness, within the group and across the broader community. Students are encouraged to think critically, work transparently, and approach problems creatively, always mindful of how their work contributes to evidence-based sustainability transitions. Building on a proven mentoring foundation, this program will continue to produce HQP who are technically proficient, reflective, and engaged researchers, capable of bridging scientific excellence with societal needs. By training students in rigorous, open, and inclusive research practices, this Discovery Grant will not only advance knowledge but also build enduring Canadian capacity for evidence-based environmental decision making.
