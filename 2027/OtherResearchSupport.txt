The present research program does not overlap with any other source of funding. It constitutes an independent and unique research project. It is highly complementary to two other sources of funding:

1) I received funding as part of the 2PURES project with the Canada Foundation for Innovation to build a common database structure and a computational infrastructure to integrate chemical engineering process modelling and circular economy analysis for plastics in the Canadian economy. Of course, this open-source digital infrastructure under development can be generalized, and will prove useful as a starting point for data management in this project.

2) I am part of the CIRAIG's International Research Consortium on Life Cycle Assessment and Sustainable Transition, which is financed by industrial and governmental partners, with activities in Canada and abroad. This source of financing supports approximately 1/3 of my graduate students, and is focussed on LCA parametrization and its use in optimization of processes and systems. We will be able to leverage some of the tools developed with this financing in the new context of this current study: scenario-anchored prospective consequential LCAs.
