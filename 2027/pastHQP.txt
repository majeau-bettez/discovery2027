=============

Since my previous Discovery Grant application (2018), I have supervised or co-supervised over 25 HQP, including 2 bachelor projects, 9 MSc students (4 as principal supervisor), 14 PhD students (10 as principal supervisor), and 2 postdoctoral fellows. Of these, all MSc students have completed their degrees, and 3 PhD students have successfully defended. Three of my MSc graduates have continued to doctoral studies, two now serve as research officers and colleagues at the CIRAIG, and one is a Manager in Sustainability Consulting at PwC Canada. Among them, one won the Best MSc Thesis in Industrial Engineering (2023), another the Best MSc Thesis in Chemical Engineering (2021) and 2nd place overall at Polytechnique Montréal. My graduated PhD students have pursued careers as a postdoctoral fellow, a research officer at CIRAIG, and a Sustainability Analyst at Unilever (UK).

My HQP consistently produce and disseminate high-quality research. Since 2018, my students have been first authors on 26 journal articles (18 published, 3 accepted, 3 under revision, 2 submitted) in leading journals such as Nature Sustainability, Journal of Industrial Ecology, and Transportation Research Part D. I am extremely proud of their work! As a supervisor, I can be quite demanding with regard to the quality of their scientific writing and the clarity of their research questions, but they have risen to the challenge and produced impactful articles that reshape the way our research field operates.

My HQPs regularly present their work at international conferences, including the International Society for Industrial Ecology meetings in Leiden and Singapore. Several have received FRQNT scholarships (×3) and other distinctions, including the Médaille du Lieutenant-Gouverneur pour la jeunesse.

Weekly 1h one-on-one meetings with each HQPs leads to extremely stimulating scientific exchanges. It allows us to get to truly know one another, and build a genuine mentorship bond that extends beyond the direct research at hand. This mentorship style was recognized when I was nominated by students for “Best Research Supervisor” at Polytechnique’s Gala Méritas (2024–2025).

My training philosophy emphasizes scientific rigor, transparency, clear communication, and intellectual independence. I encourage students to identify and frame their own research questions, but insist on clarity of purpose and concise, jargon-free writing.

I strive to provide HQP with a collaborative, interdisciplinary, and applied training environment. Through the CIRAIG International Research Consortium, my students interact twice yearly with industrial and governmental partners. These exchanges not only strengthen research relevance (“someone in the real world cares”) but also expose students to the policy and decision-making contexts their work aims to inform. Several students have also taken part in teaching and supervision of undergraduate projects, developing leadership and pedagogical skills.

Beyond technical modelling, I guide HQP toward a deeper understanding of why their research matters: to produce knowledge that is transparent, reproducible, and useful for sustainability transitions. My group’s open-science culture encourages shared codebases, collaborative writing, and data management best practices, ensuring that trainees leave with both advanced analytical capacity and a commitment to research integrity.

Overall, my record demonstrates a strong and consistent contribution to HQP training across all levels. My students publish widely, win competitive awards, and transition into impactful careers in academia, industry, and government. Equally important, they develop the intellectual autonomy, scientific clarity, and collaborative spirit that define the next generation of sustainability researchers and practitioners.
